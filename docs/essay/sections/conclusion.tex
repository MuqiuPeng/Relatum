\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented Relatum, a relation-first reasoning engine that discovers mathematical structure through two independent mechanisms---inductive closure scoring and property implication analysis---and demonstrated their cross-validation across three mathematical domains.

\subsection{Summary of Results}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Algebraic discovery}: from 19{,}683 raw operations, the system identifies abelian groups as the optimal axiom class through dual-signal alignment, with formal equivalence to standard group axioms and successful transfer to $\mathbb{Z}_4$.

\item \textbf{Property implication}: across 10 axiom classes, the system discovers that identity determines left--right equivalence, inverse determines identity--idempotent equivalence, and associativity is independent of both. On a contrasting magma, equivalences correctly fail.

\item \textbf{Cross-domain generality}: the property mechanism produces structurally analogous findings on algebra (10 classes), order theory (241 posets), and set theory (ZFC). Four meta-patterns---definitional equivalence, equivalence breaking, strict implication, degenerate collapse---repeat across all three domains (11/11 confirmed).

\item \textbf{Failure diagnosis}: the system distinguishes structural failure (genuine mathematical non-equivalence) from resource failure (derivation-rate competition) by varying the fact budget. This makes the limitation not just acknowledged but testable.

\item \textbf{Concept discovery}: property conjunction without negation yields 0 informative combinations across all domains (properties are already ``minimally complete''). Adding negation as an orthogonality generator produces genuinely new concepts: ``interior elements'' on a 4-element chain. Cross-degenerate filtering eliminates 67\% of false discoveries.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Central Claim}

The structural boundary between groups and weaker algebraic structures is independently discoverable by two mechanisms that share no intermediate results. The property implication mechanism generalizes to non-algebraic domains without modification. These findings suggest that extension-based property analysis captures a domain-independent structural invariant of mathematical reasoning.

\subsection{Future Directions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Deeper composition}: recursive composition ($p \wedge (q \wedge r)$), disjunction ($p \vee q$), and conditional properties ($p \mid q$) as additional constructors.

\item \textbf{$\omega$-rule on property implications}: promoting observed cross-structure implications to meta-level universal claims, with appropriate defenses against the risks identified in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\item \textbf{Larger carriers}: extending the algebraic enumeration beyond $n = 3$, potentially using symmetry reduction to manage the $n^{n^2}$ growth.

\item \textbf{Proto-level axiom discovery}: the beam search with compression + purity scoring autonomously discovers transitivity from raw binary relation data. Extending this to discover full axiom systems (e.g., lattice axioms from poset data) is a natural next step.
\end{itemize}
