\begin{abstract}

We present Relatum, a relation-first reasoning engine that autonomously discovers algebraic structure through two independent mechanisms: \emph{inductive closure scoring} and \emph{property implication analysis}. Starting from an exhaustive enumeration of all 19{,}683 binary operations on a 3-element carrier, the closure-scoring mechanism partitions the model space into 10 axiom classes and identifies the abelian group region as optimal through dual-signal alignment of model-space rarity and closure-space richness. A formal equivalence proof establishes that the discovered rule bundle is logically equivalent to the group axiom subset $\{G_2, G_3\}$, holding for all groups including infinite ones.

Independently, the property implication mechanism defines algebraic properties (left identity, right identity, idempotent) as first-class relational objects and discovers their pairwise equivalence or strict implication by comparing property extensions. Exhaustive verification on all 935 non-trivial 3-element models finds: (1)~identity presence is both necessary and sufficient for left--right identity equivalence (verified on 935/935 models); (2)~inverse presence guarantees the identity as the unique idempotent, but commutativity with identity alone yields this equivalence in only 75\% of models, revealing structural diversity within axiom classes that single-representative sampling cannot detect; (3)~associativity is independent of both equivalences.

The two mechanisms use entirely different inference paths---closure generativity scoring versus property extension inclusion---yet produce consistent structural classifications. This cross-validation, obtained without human guidance on what to look for, constitutes the central result: the algebraic boundary between groups and weaker structures is discoverable from raw operation tables by multiple independent methods within a single relational framework.

Beyond analysis, the system constructs new properties via conjunction and negation, scored by a combination of independence, rarity, and constraint. On a 4-element chain, it autonomously discovers the concept of ``interior elements'' ($\lnot\text{maximal} \wedge \lnot\text{minimal}$) --- a recognized mathematical concept found without being told its name. A cross-degenerate filter eliminates combinations that merely reconstruct existing properties, reducing the false discovery rate from 67\% to 0\%.

The engine also supports pattern-level deduction (variable-to-variable unification, stratified negation, $\omega$-rule inductive promotion, and proof by contradiction), demonstrated on ZFC set-theoretic axioms where the system derives universally quantified conclusions such as $\forall A.\;\varnothing \in \mathcal{P}(A)$ from two input propositions in 0.00s with 11 total facts.

\end{abstract}
