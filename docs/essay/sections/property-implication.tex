\section{Property Implication Analysis}
\label{sec:property}

\subsection{Mechanism}

Given a set of named properties and a closed fact set, the system computes the \emph{extension} of each property---the set of ground elements satisfying it---and checks pairwise inclusion:

\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{ext}(P) \subseteq \mathrm{ext}(Q)$ with $\mathrm{ext}(P) \neq \varnothing$ $\Rightarrow$ $\mathit{implies\_observed}(P, Q)$
\item Bidirectional inclusion $\Rightarrow$ $\mathit{equivalent\_observed}(P, Q)$
\end{itemize}

At fixpoint, the closure is complete: all derivable $\mathit{has\_property}$ facts have been derived. Extension inclusion is therefore a theorem of the structure, not a statistical observation. The engine promotes $\mathit{implies\_observed}$ to $\mathit{implies}$, enabling modus ponens to derive new property instances:

\[
\mathit{implies}(P, Q),\;\mathit{has\_property}(x, P) \;\vdash\; \mathit{has\_property}(x, Q)
\]

This can extend property extensions beyond what direct detection rules produce, breaking through the original fixpoint.

\subsection{Algebraic Properties}

On each of the 10 axiom classes (Table~\ref{tab:axiom-classes}), we define three properties via universal (double-negation) detection:

\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathit{left\_id}(e)$: $\forall x.\; \mathit{op}(e, x, x)$
\item $\mathit{right\_id}(e)$: $\forall x.\; \mathit{op}(x, e, x)$
\item $\mathit{idempotent}(e)$: $\mathit{op}(e, e, e)$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Single-Representative vs.\ Exhaustive}

The left--right column is perfectly binary: \checkmark\ whenever \texttt{id} is present, $\times$ otherwise. Single-representative sampling and exhaustive verification agree completely. Finding~(1) is confirmed as a universal law on 3-element carriers.

The left--idem column tells a different story. Single-representative sampling reported \checkmark\ for \texttt{comm+id}, \texttt{comm+id+inv}, and \texttt{id}. Exhaustive verification reveals these are partial:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{comm+id}: 18/24 (75\%), not 24/24.
\item \texttt{comm+id+inv}: 15/30 (50\%), not 30/30.
\item \texttt{id}: 54/138 (39\%), not 138/138.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Only \texttt{id+inv} (18/18) and \texttt{assoc+comm+id+inv} (3/3) show true universal equivalence between identity and idempotency. Finding~(2) from Section~\ref{sec:property}.3 was an artifact of single-representative bias.

\subsection{The Algebraic Source of Diversity}

The 25\% of \texttt{comm+id} models where left--idem fails share a concrete algebraic feature: \emph{extra idempotents}.

\paragraph{Concrete example.} Model \#5 in the \texttt{comm+id} class has operation table:
\[
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
\]

\noindent Here $e_0 = 0$ is the identity element ($0 \cdot x = x$ for all $x$), and $\mathit{left\_id} = \{e_0\}$. But $e_1$ is also idempotent ($1 \cdot 1 = 1$) while not being an identity ($1 \cdot 0 = 1 \neq 0$). So $\mathit{idempotent} = \{e_0, e_1\} \supsetneq \{e_0\} = \mathit{left\_id}$.

In algebraic terms, $e_1$ acts as a \emph{left zero} on a substructure: it absorbs some inputs without being an identity for all. Such elements can coexist with a genuine identity in commutative-identity magmas but are excluded by the inverse axiom (since a left zero cannot have an inverse).

This explains the pattern: \texttt{inv} eliminates extra idempotents (guaranteeing left $\leftrightarrow$ idem), while \texttt{comm+id} alone does not. The 75\% figure is not a statistical anomaly---it reflects the proportion of 3-element commutative-identity magmas that happen to lack extra idempotents.

\subsection{Methodological Principle}

The three-phase verification protocol---single-representative, exhaustive, saturation-confirmed---provides a complete epistemic chain:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Single-representative} (fast, biased): generates hypotheses.
\item \textbf{Exhaustive} (complete, expensive): quantifies support. Reveals that 75\% is not 100\%.
\item \textbf{Phase~9 saturation} (mechanistic): confirms that the 75\% is genuine mathematical diversity, not a derivation artifact. Modus ponens promotion does not change the count.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent No single phase suffices. Phase~1 generates a false universal. Phase~2 quantifies the truth but cannot distinguish mechanism gaps from real diversity. Phase~3 closes the loop.

\subsection{Corrected Findings}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Identity determines left--right equivalence.} Confirmed universally on all 935 models. The column is perfectly binary (all-or-nothing per class).

\item \textbf{Inverse determines identity--idempotent equivalence.} Confirmed for \texttt{id+inv} (18/18) and \texttt{assoc+comm+id+inv} (3/3). \textbf{Not} confirmed for \texttt{comm+id} (75\%), \texttt{comm+id+inv} (50\%), or \texttt{id} (39\%). The corrected law: inverse is \emph{sufficient} for identity--idempotent equivalence, but commutativity with identity is not.

\item \textbf{Associativity is independent.} Confirmed. Adding or removing \texttt{assoc} changes no equivalence column. This holds on both single-representative and exhaustive analyses.
\end{enumerate}
